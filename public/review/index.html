Design question

How can a learner practise the reasoning behind a repair?

A static diagram can name a component, but it does not show the consequences of an action. This prototype connects visible water behaviour with an explicit sequence of observations, decisions, and checks.

The scope is deliberately small: a generic gravity-fed cistern with a float-controlled inlet and flapper-style outlet. Three fixed scenarios exercise persistent water loss, overflow, and absent refill. Exploration, guided practice, and independent assessment share the same model.

Teaching prototype only. The model has not been reviewed by a repair professional or validated against physical equipment. It is neither general repair guidance nor a digital twin or vocational assessment.

Implementation choices

Three decisions that shape the system

The emphasis is on inspectable behaviour,
not visual complexity.

01

One state model, several views

A typed reducer owns the attempt: supply, water level, component assembly, evidence, diagnosis, errors, and retest. The 3D scene presents that state. Camera controls, cutaway, explosion, and workspace expansion are separate display choices.

Why it mattersA camera movement cannot silently fix a fault or change a score.

02

Rejected actions remain part of the record

Disassembly checks the model’s preconditions. If they are missing, the component stays installed and the rejected action is logged. Critical safety errors survive refresh and remain visible in the final report.

Why it mattersA numeric score of 100 does not override a critical safety failure.

03

Verification is part of the interaction

Repair alone does not complete an attempt. A controlled simulation step drives the working cycle; the result must satisfy the applicable assembly, supply, stable-water, and refill checks. Scoring is computed from the attempt record.

Why it mattersSuccess follows observable state and completed checks, not a “next” button.

A five-minute review route

Try one complete guided case

Launch the app ↗

This walkthrough gives the answer for the no-refill case only. Use guided practice to inspect the complete interaction. Chinese labels below match the actual controls. Allow the automatic retest to finish.

  1. Choose the case and mode

    On the course page, select 03 排水后没有补水 (no refill after draining), then 开始引导训练 (start guided practice).

  2. Collect three observations

    Click 查看水位 (check water level) and 检查供水 (check supply). Select 05 进水组件 (inlet assembly) in the left part list, then click 记录部件观察 (record observation).

  3. Submit the diagnosis

    In 选择诊断 (select diagnosis), choose 供水阀关闭 (supply valve closed), then click 提交诊断 (submit).

  4. Restore supply and verify

    Open the 模拟处理 (simulated treatment) tab. Click 恢复供水 (restore supply); watch the 3D water level rise. Click 运行复测 (run retest) and wait for 复测通过 (retest passed). This case needs no replacement.

  5. Inspect the result and retained history

    Click 结束并查看报告 (finish and view report) and confirm in the dialog. Review the score breakdown, then refresh or open 学习记录 (learning history). Guided practice does not issue a completion certificate.

Scope & contribution

An inspectable prototype,
with explicit limits.

AI-assisted development. Codex assisted with implementation, iteration, testing, and documentation in response to the project owner’s requirements. The repository records the resulting system and validation evidence; it is not evidence of unaided authorship.

Local and illustrative. Records are stored in the browser and are editable. A local demonstration certificate is not an accredited qualification or evidence of occupational competence. No backend, login, payment, live AI service, or real-world repair validation is included.

Next evaluation step. Domain-expert review and a small, consented usability study would be needed before claiming educational value. Physical model calibration and device testing remain separate engineering tasks.
